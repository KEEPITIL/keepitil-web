KEEPITIL PRESENTS

RAVE GOD — The High Priest of Rave Core

Hard techno, sultry house, and a sound he branded himself — rave core. A Huntington Beach DJ who came up from the Los Angeles underground floor in 2025 and now holds the 3 a.m. slot at the 18th Annual Haunt Massive.

KEEPITIL

Some DJ names are marketing. RAVE GOD’s is a job description he wrote for his crowd as much as himself — he opens his posts by greeting his “rave gods and goddesses,” and then he goes out and plays like the title has to be re-earned every night. This Halloween, the earning happens at the gravest slot on the bill: 3 a.m. at the 18th Annual Haunt Massive, Mayhem Events’ long-running Halloween rave, whose ticket page lists 1735 Vine St in Los Angeles. For a Huntington Beach DJ who, by the event’s own artist blurb, only became a DJ in 2025, a 3 a.m. Halloween set is not an early-career footnote. It’s the headline of the story so far.

The blurb Haunt Massive is running for him reads like a mission statement: a hard techno DJ from Huntington Beach whose sets are built “to entertain the realest ravers,” with “an energetic, crowd-engaging stage presence.” A year and a half of underground receipts — warehouse afters, a residency, headline day parties, a three-show festival week — says the blurb is not bluffing.

A raver first

RAVE GOD’s origin is the floor, not the booth. Per the Haunt Massive artist blurb, he is “a raver who fell in love with the Los Angeles underground scene” before he became a DJ in 2025. The paper trail backs the timeline: by June 2025 he was already playing out — an open-decks set at the Yost Theater for Electric Jungle (thanking @novacanesounds for the shot), a Long Beach pop-up with @thirdeyebadtripp, and a beach rave in his own Huntington Beach for a visiting church group from Arizona, where he delivered “a proper rave god house set.” Open decks, pop-ups, a beach party for out-of-towners: that is a first-year DJ saying yes to every room that would have him, in the order underground careers actually happen.

The through-line since has been loyalty to the collectives that put him on. He thanks them by name, constantly, and they keep re-booking him — which, in a scene with no shortage of DJs, is the metric that matters.

The sound: rave core, and a second gear

Ask him what he plays and he has a branded answer. In his own words, from the caption of his Wasteland Recovery Party set post:

“And what’s rave core, you ask? It’s all the hard techno I love to play, including hardcore, hard bounce, hard psy trance, hard dance, and Schranz, plus dubstep and riddim — and all of it fits a fun, dark, dancey, and most importantly ravey vibe.”

That set ran at 162 BPM — his own figure, from the reel announcing the recording. The full Wasteland Recovery Party set is up on SoundCloud (linked from his Instagram bio), and it is the cleanest single document of the sound: hard, fast, and deliberately fun where a lot of hard techno settles for grim.

But rave core is only one of his two gears. His Instagram bio bills him as “hard techno | sultry house,” and the sultry house side is real: hybrid house-and-techno sets where he weaves dubstep sections into techno frameworks — a technique he has written about learning deliberately, “to keep the crowd engaged.” His KEEPITIL application lists his genres plainly as hard techno and tech house, and the bookings split the difference: the same DJ who plays 174 BPM hard techno in a ring (his Phase One LA debut, May 2026) also headlines daytime house sessions in Huntington Beach.

The rooms

The résumé reads like a map of the SoCal underground’s working circuit:

  • TeckMeOut is his most frequent room — an afters collective he plays again and again, in the deep-late slots (a 3:20–4:20 a.m. set in October 2026; “my first Sunday night with them” earlier in the season). His October recap calls them “an amazing collective that’s believed in me.”
  • Duck Pond LA made him a resident — it’s in his bio — and hosted the debut of his back-to-back project with @blerrydj in May 2026, an extended set of “hard bounce, hardcore, and hard psy.” He calls Duck Pond “a collective that stays true to the underground and keeps rave culture alive.”
  • Electric Jungle gave him early stages (the Yost open decks in 2025) and a headline night — “rave god & friends” at Yost Nightlife, presented by Electric Jungle and Pure Light, where he played on behalf of “two collectives that put me on and hold me down.”
  • Kastle Dungeon gave him his hard techno rite of passage — his words: “for this underground rite of passage I made sure as always to keep the playlist to bangers only.”
  • Proof Bar gave him a takeover: a “rave god Takeover” night where he brought Richrunthat and Mami Crvsh with him and ran open decks from 10–11 p.m. for anyone brave enough — headliner as host, building the next rung for other DJs.
  • Kingdom at Catch One (August 2026, for We Love Kandy SoCal and Grooveism) is where the showman fully shows up: he turned the Fizzle Stage into “the Inferno Stage,” worked the mic as his own MC, and — his caption again — “I love making you jump.” It was his first time leaving the booth to dance with the crowd.
  • The Foam Rave (Climax Events LA × Ravela LA, late August 2026) put him on opening duties for one of the summer’s biggest productions. Watching the foam build a ceiling over the crowd through the lasers was, he wrote, “easily one of my favorite memories as a raver yet” — note the word. Even on stage, he still files the memory as a raver.

Then came Wasteland week in September 2026, the clearest proof of workload: three shows in four days. Thursday, hard techno on a rooftop at Somewhere Special LA. Saturday, Beats At The Park next to Dodger Stadium — hosted by Casual Hippies, KEEPITIL, and Teknlgy — which means RAVE GOD has already played a KEEPITIL stage. Sunday, the Wasteland Recovery Party in DTLA for Tech My Warehouse LA × Hard Havoc, the set that became his SoundCloud calling card. Add day-party headline slots — Cruisers House Sessions in Huntington Beach on Saturday, headlining Helipad Society, “the underground’s most consistent weekly outdoor event,” in DTLA on Sunday — and the picture is a DJ being trusted with every slot type the underground has: opener, resident, takeover host, day-party headliner, and 3 a.m. afters closer.

The work behind the decks

Two details separate him from the pack of talented first-years. First, his Instagram bio lists a second job: “Bus. & Leg. Affairs @6host.records” — business and legal affairs for 6HOST Records. The DJ who plays the underground also does paperwork for a label in it, which says something about how seriously he takes the business side of a scene that runs on handshakes.

Second, he is building the production side: a January 2026 reel documents him in the studio making a track with @hotpursuitdj. No releases are public yet that we could verify — but the direction is set: the sets are the engine, and original music is the obvious next cylinder.

He is also, not incidentally, KEEPITIL family by application and by stage time: his DJ application was the very first one in the book (March 24, 2026 — he applied twice, in March and again in June), and he has already played a KEEPITIL-hosted event at Beats At The Park. This feature has been a long time coming; he was, literally, first in line.

Why book RAVE GOD

  • A defined, branded sound — “rave core” (hardcore, hard bounce, hard psy, hard dance, Schranz, dubstep/riddim) at festival tempo, with a genuine second gear of sultry house and tech house
  • Proven across every slot: resident (Duck Pond LA), afters closer (3:20–4:20 a.m. at TeckMeOut), opener for major productions (Foam Rave), day-party headliner (Helipad Society, Cruisers House Sessions)
  • A headline Halloween booking: 3 a.m. at the 18th Annual Haunt Massive (Mayhem Events)
  • Stage presence that leaves the booth — MC work, crowd work, and a set recording on SoundCloud so bookers can hear exactly what they’re getting
  • Scene infrastructure, not just a guest: business & legal affairs at 6HOST Records, open-decks host at his own takeover, loyal repeat relationships with the collectives that book him

The quick version

RAVE GOD (Michael Hanna) is a Huntington Beach hard techno and tech house DJ who came up from the Los Angeles underground floor in 2025 and, inside two years, stacked a Duck Pond LA residency, repeat afters bookings at TeckMeOut, a three-show Wasteland week (including a KEEPITIL-hosted stage), an opening set at the Climax × Ravela Foam Rave, and a 3 a.m. Halloween slot at the 18th Annual Haunt Massive. His sound — “rave core,” plus a sultry house second gear — is documented on SoundCloud, and his next set is always the point: he plays to entertain the realest ravers in the room.
