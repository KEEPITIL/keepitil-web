KEEPITIL PRESENTS

TBH-ThatBoiHarris — A 216 Kid Running Bass House on the Orange County Circuit

Cleveland on the application, Browns 13 on his back — and a working DJ’s résumé across Orange County’s underground rooms.

KEEPITIL

In February 2026, TBH-ThatBoiHarris posted a three-clip carousel from a bright home studio and captioned it like a man clocking back in: “Back in the function. I appreciate everyone that has contributed to my journey as well as stood by me during hard times…. never switched up on me but instead be someone I can count on as a friend. With school music and life moving…..Man what can I say I mean TBH….it’s go time.” The clips show the uniform he plays in — red cap, red Beats headphones, and a Cleveland Browns jersey with the number 13 — hands working a Pioneer controller through a high-energy house cut, a moodier vocal pass, and a straight instrumental run. It is a comeback post, a skills tape and a mission statement in one swipe, and the jersey is not a costume. It is the whole story in one garment: a Cleveland kid, doing his work two time zones from home.

TBH-ThatBoiHarris — Blake Harris — is a house and bass house DJ whose application to KEEPITIL lists Cleveland, and whose receipts list just about everywhere in Orange County. He is, on paper, the only non-California artist in this queue. In practice, as the record below shows, he has been playing the same OC rooms as the California applicants for years — sometimes on the same bill, the same night, for the same promoter.

Two accounts, one DJ

Harris runs his life across two Instagrams, and the split is deliberate. The main account, @looslylaced — “Sneakerfreaker31,” 2,092 followers — is the person: its bio reads “Work hard Catch the vibes 🤪🤪 dj page -@tbh_thatboiharris GAMER🎮. DJ🎶 Biker🏍️ physical therapy student , MBAgrad📚📓📖 ,athlete,♏️”. The DJ page, @tbh_thatboiharris, is the business card, and its bio is a one-line booking pitch: “Bass house,tech house ,house DJ -ThatBoiHarris(Tbh)main account @looslylaced.”

That division of labor tells you how he operates. The DJ page carries the flyers, the set announcements and the performance clips — 18 posts of almost pure work. The main page carries everything the work is built on top of: the gym years, the motorcycle, the tattoos, the degree, the comeback. Promoters booking the DJ page get the DJ. The main page is where you learn why the dance floor trusts him: he posts his life the way he posts his sets, with nothing cut out.

The sound

The application says House/Basshouse. His own bio widens it by one word — bass house, tech house, house — and his captions fill in the temperament. A February 2025 carousel of him at work is captioned, in full, “Spinnnin that bee boop bop musiccc😜😜” — which, for a genre description, is honestly hard to improve on: bouncy, wobbly, grinning bass house, played like it is supposed to be fun. His hashtags run the same way — #housemusiclovers, #djlifestyle, #edclv, #techno — and when his feed wanders off-genre it wanders toward more energy, not less: a July 2026 reel shouts out Sub Focus and tags drum and bass.

The production chapter is announced, on the record, and dated. On January 2, 2025, he posted the plan to his main page: “Took alot of L”s in 2024 but it was to make room for my 2025 W”s that are ganna happen. Production is in play and you will hear my personality and what I feel in every track can’t wait.” No public SoundCloud, Mixcloud or Spotify profile surfaced in research for this feature — so for now, the TBH sound lives where his audience already is: in the clips, the carousels, and the rooms below. What those clips show is a DJ who demos range on purpose — the February 2026 carousel deliberately runs a peak-time house vocal, a melancholic melodic pass and an instrumental workout back to back, a three-mood calling card cut for promoters scrolling fast.

The circuit

Here is where the “Cleveland artist” framing meets the flyers. On June 6, 2025, his DJ page posted the night’s plan: “Tonight come to @topfloordtsa 9pm-2am for banging house music and lit DJs put on by @savage.ent420: @miintycatt @imravegod @dj_rab3l_r3mix @tmzee_ @ogliljacuzzi @tbh_thatboiharris — Free entry all night!” Top Floor is downtown Santa Ana’s underground room, Savage Entertainment is fellow KEEPITIL artist Smoke’s company, and the bill he shared that night included RAVE GOD and RAB3L — two other artists in this very Connect series. The out-of-towner framing collapses right there: TBH was already inside the OC scene’s core room, on a core bill, a full year before he applied.

The rest of the circuit is the working DJ’s Orange County. In February 2025 he was posting the call for a Friday of house music all night long at The Continental Room in Fullerton — the same room Smoke’s reels rave about. On November 30, 2025, the post was pure Sunday-service energy: “Catch me tonight at sandbar for some Sunday funday vibes 🤙🏽🤪. #huntingtonbeach” — Sandbar, Huntington Beach, a Sunday funday set. An August 2025 post promoted an open-decks night in downtown Los Angeles — “Beat the clock, skip the cover. ⏰🎟️ Open Decks 8–9PM 🎧 … Free before 9PM, $15 after. RSVP in bio.” — and his reels from that same stretch share the recap of Mukti’s first Hollywood night, all underground LA DJs and massive visuals.

Los Angeles became a headline in its own right in 2026. Posting ahead of a September 5 date, he wrote: “Come vibe with me sept 5th 😌. Only been to LA a few times(and I live in oc lol ) so this is ganna be a set for the books 🤓🤓 love yall 🫶🏽🫶🏽🫶🏽” — the “(and I live in oc lol)” doing more to locate him than any bio line. A week later came September 12 with Drop The Pin Collective: “Got some fly events coming up with even more fly people to meet. Come check out @dropthepin.cllctv as we cook up some music for yall to vibe with 😜😜.” Drop The Pin — the clothes-swap-plus-live-DJs community format — is the same LA collective that runs through Smoke’s orbit; TBH’s reel from the night bills it as a debut, with “a Clothes swap section, vendors, non-stop activities, & live DJ’s.”

And then there is the booking that ties him directly to this platform. In July 2026 he was promoting KEEP IT IL SUMMER — KEEPITIL’s own beach day at Huntington Beach: “House music, Afrobeats, and hip hop on the sand at Huntington Beach. Free, family-friendly vibes all day on Sunday, July 12 from 12 PM to 8 PM,” with his own post the day before pointing followers to the RSVP link: “We outsideeee ☀️☀️☀️ let’s get the summer vibes 🙏🙏🙏.” He applied to KEEPITIL on June 23, 2026, and within three weeks he was on the platform’s summer bill, on the sand, in his adopted home county. That is not an applicant waiting to be discovered. That is a working DJ the paperwork caught up with.

Around the gigs runs the festival spine you would expect from his generation of house DJ: his June 2025 posts roll through Day Trip Festival in Long Beach, and the hashtag run on his life-dump carousel reads like a SoCal season pass — #edc #beyondwonderland #daytripfestival #escapehalloween — with an August 2025 Palm Springs reel from Splash House season captioned “Let’s rock 2nd half 😈😈😈🫶🏽🥵.” He is a fan in the crowd at the big ones and a name on the flyer at the local ones, and the feed never pretends otherwise.

The 216 thread

So what about Cleveland? The roots are his own, stated in his own shorthand. A November 2021 gym reel — from the deep archive of his main page, all Newport Beach and Costa Mesa hashtags — signs off “216 kid.” 216 is Cleveland’s area code, and he wears the city the same way: the Browns #13 jersey is his performance uniform, front and center in both his DJ-page profile photo (on the decks, blue-lit, one hand on the headphones) and the February 2026 comeback carousel. His application lists Cleveland. His bookings phone, as submitted, is a 714 number — Orange County. Both things are on the record, and the article isn’t going to resolve what he hasn’t: the honest read is a Cleveland-raised DJ who built his working life in Orange County and reps both, the jersey at the decks doing the talking.

The person behind the decks

The main page’s bio is a résumé in emoji, and every line of it checks out in the posts. Physical therapy student — and, already, an MBA graduate. Athlete: the 2021 archive is a serious gym log, post after post of Newport, Costa Mesa and Huntington Beach iron, including the stat line he posted himself — “240lbs 6”5” — a 6-foot-5 frame that makes him easy to spot behind a booth. Biker: an October 2025 post debuts his motorcycle — “Somethin new” love this death rocket lol 😂 — tagged #ruffryders. Gamer, per the bio. Scorpio, per the bio. Tattooed, per the June 2025 carousel out of Anaheim showing off his ink — anime, Marvel and Demon Slayer pieces among them.

The most-shared chapter is the transformation. In May 2026 he posted the result of a year and a half of quiet work: “Life has been wild the last year and a half so I just locked in , put my life on dnd and just kept my nose down . Wild -68 lb drop and pre surgery (next month for my arm ) but this is ganna stay like this per diet . Let’s rock ittttt. To the people who held me down a continue to do so this is bigger then me an i appreciate the love and support. ❤️❤️❤️🙌🙌” — signed, simply, “-TBH.” A 68-pound drop, posted not as a before-and-after ad but as a thank-you note. It rhymes with the September 2025 post beside it: “Wild , I just been working on a lot internally and the results have been so positive. … Gods always got a plan for people and puts you around amazing people that share so much of the same light.”

Even his motivational posting is specific. The one long quote on his main page, attached to a March 2025 photo carousel shot by @djbalvarez, is Steve Jobs: “Life can be much broader once you discover one simple fact: Everything around you that you call life was made up by people that were no smarter than you. And you can change it, you can influence it. … Once you learn that, you’ll never be the same again.” For a DJ who is simultaneously in physical-therapy school, holding an MBA, rebuilding his body and building a booking sheet, it is less a poster quote than a syllabus. His own coinage for the lifestyle is better anyway — since 2025 his posts have promised “the next litivites,” and a reel last October welcomed followers in properly: “Welcome to lil oh TBH”s littivaties lol 😜😜😜.”

Why book TBH-ThatBoiHarris

  • Two coasts of story in one booking: Cleveland roots he wears on his back (the Browns #13 is his stage uniform; “216 kid” is his own sign-off) with a working Orange County base — a headline angle no other DJ in this series carries
  • Already room-tested on the OC underground circuit: Top Floor DTSA on a Savage Entertainment bill (June 6, 2025, alongside RAVE GOD and RAB3L), The Continental Room (Fullerton), Sandbar (Huntington Beach) Sunday funday, DTLA open decks, and LA dates with Drop The Pin Collective (Sept 5 + Sept 12, 2026)
  • A KEEPITIL name already: applied June 23, 2026 and was promoting the platform’s KEEP IT IL SUMMER beach day at Huntington Beach (July 12, 2026) within the month
  • The sound is the room’s sweet spot: bass house / tech house / house, demoed on his page in three moods (peak-time, melodic, instrumental), with production announced in his own words since January 2025 — “you will hear my personality and what I feel in every track”
  • A promoter’s poster, not just a player: at 6’5” with an athlete’s presence, an MBA’s professionalism, and a two-account setup (2,092-follower main page + dedicated DJ page) that pushes every flyer twice

The quick version

TBH-ThatBoiHarris (Blake Harris) is a house and bass house DJ who applied to KEEPITIL from Cleveland — “216 kid,” Browns #13 at the decks — and works the Orange County circuit his posts document: Top Floor DTSA with Savage Entertainment, The Continental Room, Sandbar in Huntington Beach, DTLA open decks, Drop The Pin in LA, and KEEPITIL’s own KEEP IT IL SUMMER beach day. A physical-therapy student and MBA graduate who runs a 2,092-follower main page beside his dedicated DJ page, he announced original production in January 2025 and a full comeback in February 2026: “TBH….it’s go time.”
