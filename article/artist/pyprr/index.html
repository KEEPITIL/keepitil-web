KEEPITIL PRESENTS

PYPRR — Hard Kicks, Punk Heart

A Pomona punk who learned to make hard techno sing — 170bpm kicks, a self-built Pomona night, and a bag that holds jersey club and hyperpop too.

KEEPITIL

Her Instagram bio asks one question: “do u wanna dance?” It is less a bio than a booking condition. PYPRR — Stevie Piper Mendez — is the Pomona-raised, Los Angeles-listed producer and DJ whose sets run at the hard end of the dial: hard techno, hardcore, rawstyle and hard dance on the flyers, with detours into jersey club, latincore and hyperpop when the room calls for them. In Las Vegas, announcing her Sin City debut, she put the offer plainly: “who’s ready for an hour of 170bpm hard kicks?” That is the product. The surprise, documented in interview after interview, is how much else she can fold into it.

The punk thread is where her own scene starts the story. Fr3akquency OC, announcing her as a resident, introduced her this way: “PYPRR is a producer and dj born and raised in Pomona, CA. Originally from the punk scene, she eventually found a love for edm and started to DJ and eventually produce. Now an emerging force in the underground scene, PYPRR brings an ensemble of hypnotizing grooves, dark melodies, and heavy kicks that make her sets a can’t miss.” MISFITS Los Angeles, the zine that profiled her for its Local Grrl in Motion series, framed the same conversation around “punk roots, hard dance, experimentation, Slipknot remixes, and building a sound that colors outside the lines” — and pulled the line that works as her DJ philosophy: “great DJs play what you didn’t know you wanted to hear.”

The sound: hard at the core, restless at the edges

Her KEEPITIL application lists hard techno and hardcore, and the warehouse receipts back the hardest version of that claim. At VICIOUS, the Fever × After Midnight × Los Perros warehouse rave in downtown Los Angeles on May 3, 2025, she opened a bill billed as “HARD TECHNO ALL NIGHT.” At TEKNOCLUB in Orange County she shared a lineup with fellow KEEPITIL applicant RAVE GOD. Pinkspoon put her in its Sauna for a hard techno mix on YouTube — her “pinksaunasession” debut — and Divergent Collective, the Pomona collective whose first anniversary she plays at Breach, had her “making the room go feral on Divergent Radio Episode 08.”

But the same feed keeps widening the frame. COMMON LOVE’s artist spotlight for its Lust Stage put it in one sentence: “Her sets seamlessly blend hard dance, jersey club, and techno in a manner that keeps the dance floor pumping.” A COMMON LOVE night at DBA 256 billed her under “latincore, hyperpop, jersey club.” A Sinergy & VibeJunkies Sunday at Projekt Redlands ran house, techno and bass with open decks billed as hardgroove. And in a completely different register, she is a recurring DJ at Yuri Rave — at Los Globos, at El Cid on Sunset — a girlypop night where the brief is favourite pop songs, contests and Pride-month celebration, including a sold-out edition she announced herself: “Playing for @yuri.rave tonight… and we’re SOLD OUT!!” A hard-techno resident who can also carry a sold-out pop rave is not a contradiction in her catalogue; it is the catalogue.

The circuit: Pomona is home, the map keeps growing

If one room anchors the story, it is DBA 256 in Pomona. That is where she launched “PYPRR & Friends” — “I’m bringing some of my best friends out to the @dba256 for my first ever PYPRR & Friends!” — and then brought it back, again and again: “PYPRR & Friends is back,” “house & techno all night, no cover, 21+,” “techno all night.” A recurring self-built night, in her home city, with no cover, is the underground’s oldest career strategy, and she is running it in the open.

From that base the map spreads in every direction a SoCal underground DJ can spread. Pomona again: Breach Fridays at Maxximo in downtown Pomona, where she plays the Divergent Collective one-year anniversary on October 23 and where Breach’s first-ever Halloween party, Breach After Dark, bills her as celebrating her birthday on the lineup. Los Angeles: closing sets for Lick N Dip — Pride closer, Sunday closer (“im back at lickndip closing out the night”), a 2–3 a.m. Sunday slot — plus her Los Perros debut (“playing some fun bouncy stuff & a lil acid tonight”), a Fever debut opening the night with “hard hitting techno,” the Countdown NYE pre-party at 414 W Pico Blvd, and an opening slot for headliner Kristyle alongside fellow KEEPITIL artist Chlophonic, on a night pitched as “deep textures, hypnotic pulses, and industrial elegance.” The Inland Empire: closing the indoor stage at Projekt Redlands for Planet Techno California. Orange County: TEKNOCLUB, a Speakeasy OC techno afters, and the Space Taco return to the Fox Theater Pomona rooftop, where she wrote she was “absolutely psyched to be added to this insane lineup of heavy hitters.” And then the marker every regional DJ wants: “LAS VEGAS !!! … don’t miss my sin city debut,” an hour of 170bpm hard kicks, booked through R4dioheav3n.

The slots tell their own story about trust. She opens rooms (Fever, VICIOUS, the Kristyle night), she closes them (Lick N Dip, Projekt Redlands), and she headlines her own recurring night. Promoters keep handing her the two hours that decide whether a night works.

The producer half

“Producer and DJ” is the order her resident bio uses, and the production record is on Spotify under her own name. Her bio’s single link points at “prodigy.,” her 2025 collaboration with ELSEWHERE — a 5:06 track from the album JUMP FROM PLANET TO PLANET (February 4, 2025), credited ©/℗ 2025 Stevie Mendez and Alma Celeste Michel. Spotify’s popular-tracks list for PYPRR leads with “bye !” (3:00), “Freak” (4:24) and “mini skirt” (4:01), all 2025 singles, with further 2025 releases listed including the album FEELINGS. The catalogue is small and current — a producer in her release year, not a legacy act — and it is growing in the same direction as the DJ bookings: the Divergent Collective feed credits a “Pyprr – (Unreleased ID)” in its album-release event at DBA 256, and Pyprr is a named mixer, alongside BlckSpin, of Divergent’s first mixtape, Divergent And Friends, released February 14, 2026: “Mixed by BlckSpin and Pyprr and produced by local and foreign artist, Divergent will be bringing you the most fluent sounds from the Underground Scene.”

There is also a second Elsewhere thread worth a booker’s attention: an Instagram interview series, @im.elsewhere, introduced her with “@_pyprr found @im.elsewhere, an interview series based on human connection and conversation. Who are you when you find yourself elsewhere?” Between the MISFITS feature, the Sauna session, the Divergent Radio episode and the interview series, she is building the press trail most DJs at her level do not have yet.

Why book PYPRR

  • Hard-end credibility with receipts: 170bpm hard-kick sets, a hard techno Sauna mix, the VICIOUS warehouse bill (“HARD TECHNO ALL NIGHT,” DTLA), TEKNOCLUB (OC) and a Las Vegas debut — hard techno / hardcore per her application, rawstyle / hard dance per her resident billing
  • A self-built draw: “PYPRR & Friends,” her recurring no-cover night at DBA 256 in Pomona, returned across multiple editions — she brings a room of her own, not just a set
  • Range promoters actually use: hard dance / jersey club / techno (COMMON LOVE Lust Stage spotlight), latincore / hyperpop bills, hardgroove Sundays, and sold-out girlypop raves (Yuri Rave, Los Globos) — one booking covers several formats
  • Slot-proof: opener (Fever debut, Kristyle support), closer (Lick N Dip, Projekt Redlands indoor stage), and peak-time resident sets (Fr3akquency OC resident announcement)
  • A producer with current releases: “prodigy.” with ELSEWHERE (2025), “bye !,” “Freak,” “mini skirt,” the FEELINGS album (2025), and a co-mixer credit on Divergent Collective’s Divergent And Friends mixtape (Feb 14, 2026)
  • Press-ready: a full Local Grrl in Motion feature (MISFITS Los Angeles) built around her punk roots and experimentation

The quick version

PYPRR (Stevie Piper Mendez) is a Pomona-born producer and DJ, listed from Los Angeles, who came out of the punk scene and now works the hard end of the underground — hard techno, hardcore, rawstyle and hard dance — with jersey club, latincore and hyperpop in the same bag. A Fr3akquency OC resident and the builder of the recurring PYPRR & Friends night at DBA 256, she plays across Pomona, Los Angeles, Orange County and the Inland Empire, with a Las Vegas debut behind her, closing and opening slots for Lick N Dip, Fever, Los Perros and Breach, and a 2025 Spotify catalogue led by “prodigy.” (with ELSEWHERE), “bye !,” “Freak” and “mini skirt.”
