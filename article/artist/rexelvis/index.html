KEEPITIL PRESENTS

Rex Elvis — The Resident Under the Disco Ball

Lake Forest’s resident curator — tacos, tequila, house music under the disco ball, and Redline Records’ first-ever spotlight.

KEEPITIL

Some DJs build a career one guest slot at a time. Rex Elvis — Rex Elvis Krohnfeldt, of Lake Forest — built his the other way: he became part of the furniture, in the best sense, at the rooms closest to home, until the rooms started billing him as the reason to come. His Instagram bio is two lines long — an eagle and a tag, “@redlinerecords.us” — and that economy is the brand. The fuller picture is in the flyers other people post about him: resident curator at Our Place in Lake Forest, selected curator at Kitsch Bar, the first artist ever spotlighted by Redline Records, and a DJ whose home-room formula is so established that the venue writes it like a recipe: “Tacos, tequila, house music. The formula that started it all.”

That formula is Taco Tuesday at Our Place Lake Forest (24301 Muirlands Blvd), and Rex is one of its resident curators — the venue’s own words. When Our Place closed out 2025, its announcement read: “Tomorrow we end off 2025 with a classic. Tacos, tequila, house music. The formula that started it all. This week our resident curators set the tone going into the New Year. We will see you once again under the disco ball.” Two weeks earlier the same room billed him by name: “From returning hard hitting residents like @rex.elvis_ to an indisputably talented b3b known as @3petedubz, you’ll want to be there… Stay close to home tomorrow and join us under the disco ball!” — December 9, 8:30 p.m. to 1:30 a.m., in Lake Forest. A resident who gets name-checked in the venue’s own copy, in his own city, is holding the most durable job in club music.

The sound: minimal and techno on paper, deep and tech in the room

His KEEPITIL application lists his genres as Minimal / Ghetto-Tech / Techno — the lean, stripped end of the spectrum. The nights he is actually billed on tell the matching story from the room’s side. The Microdose & Redline Records takeover at Kitsch Bar — Friday, November 21, 9 p.m. to 2 a.m., free with RSVP — was billed in three words: “DEEP | TECH | MINIMAL HOUSE,” and its copy called Microdose and Redline “2 OF ORANGE COUNTY’S POWERHOUSE COLLECTIVES… BRINGING YOU SOME OF THE FINEST HOUSE MUSIC.” His own feed’s shorthand is looser and more honest about the job: “beep boops & outlawing jaeger,” “Some epic knob turning with @d.murrayy67,” and, after a long one, “Played an overnight set fuck me im tired.” The overnight set is the tell — minimal and techno DJs earn their keep in the long hours, and Rex is on record doing exactly that shift.

The collective: Redline Records’ first spotlight

The one affiliation Rex claims publicly is Redline Records — it is the only tag in his bio — and Redline claimed him back, first. Redline Records, an Orange County house and techno events company and record label (“House + Techno Events / Record Label”), launched its artist spotlight series with him: “Artist Spotlight 001 featuring Rex Elvis.” First in the series is a small fact with a clear meaning: when the label introduced its roster to the world, he was the introduction.

The Redline connection runs through his circuit, too. Redline’s current bio link is its Halloween Boiler Room at Tango Laguna, and Rex’s feed carries the call for a Boiler Room set rave at @tangolaguna. In November 2025 the two sides of his circuit met on one bill: Microdose — the OC house collective — and Redline Records took over Kitsch Bar together, deep, tech and minimal house all night. And when Kitsch Bar brought its local selectors back, Rex was on the curator line again, billed as a “Selected Curator” alongside @thirdeyegrimemusic and @shanexyoung: “This Friday, we return to Kitsch Bar. A lineup close to home, with a few familiar faces and some new local selectors ready to keep the room moving all night… See you under the disco ball. NO COVER, 21+.”

On camera, and beyond Lake Forest

For a DJ with a two-line bio, Rex has an unusual amount of interview tape. QueRiquisssTV caught him at work, at home: “Beats, Tacos, & Heavy Bass: Catching up with DJ @rex.elvis_ on Taco Tuesday at @ourplacelakeforest.” Hatfish TV put him on camera as well, promising its audience he “didn’t hold back.” That is the profile of a local figure, not a hired hand — the kind of resident a neighbourhood scene interviews because he is part of its identity.

The circuit extends past Lake Forest when the calendar calls for it. His feed carries the EDC Recovery Afters in downtown Los Angeles — “From the speedway straight to the warehouse. Techno • Bass • House • Everything in between. Free w/ RSVP” — and Oasis in Downey (8830 Apollo Way), a night built from “talent from OC, LA & the IE,” pitched as “No gimmicks. Just proper house music, good drinks, and the best space to spend your Saturday night.” Proper house music, no gimmicks: as a one-line summary of Rex Elvis’s whole booking proposition, the Oasis copywriters accidentally wrote his artist bio.

Away from the decks, his feed is surf, mopeds and mates — “Mopeds n shit,” “Mahalo n shit,” “I luv my friends <3” — and one piece of craft worth noting: a first self-made photo collage he titled “Controlled Chaos,” captioned “Put a collage together for my first time! LMK what you think.” Controlled chaos is also, not coincidentally, a fair description of a good minimal set.

Why book Rex Elvis

  • A proven resident, not a guest: resident curator at Our Place Lake Forest’s Taco Tuesday — the room’s own copy names him among its “hard hitting residents” and hands its resident curators the New Year send-off slot (8:30 p.m.–1:30 a.m. under the disco ball)
  • Label-backed: the only affiliation in his bio is Redline Records, and Redline made him its Artist Spotlight 001 — the first artist the OC house/techno label chose to introduce
  • Minimal/tech fluency: his application genres are Minimal / Ghetto-Tech / Techno, and he played the Microdose × Redline takeover at Kitsch Bar billed “DEEP | TECH | MINIMAL HOUSE” (Nov 21, 9 p.m.–2 a.m.), returning to Kitsch as a named Selected Curator
  • Long-set stamina: on record playing an overnight set — the shift minimal and techno bills are built on
  • A local draw with a face: interviewed on camera at his own residency (QueRiquisssTV, “Beats, Tacos, & Heavy Bass”) and by Hatfish TV — a recognisable Lake Forest figure, not an anonymous selector
  • Regional reach when needed: EDC Recovery Afters (DTLA) and Oasis (Downey) put him on OC/LA/IE bills beyond his home room

The quick version

Rex Elvis (Rex Elvis Krohnfeldt) is a Lake Forest DJ working the minimal, ghetto-tech and techno end of the spectrum, best known as a resident curator of Taco Tuesday at Our Place Lake Forest — “tacos, tequila, house music” under the disco ball — and as Redline Records’ Artist Spotlight 001. A Selected Curator at Kitsch Bar, where he played the Microdose × Redline deep/tech/minimal takeover, with an overnight set and DTLA and Downey afters bills on the record, he is the home-room resident as a booking proposition: local, durable, and already part of the furniture in the best sense.
