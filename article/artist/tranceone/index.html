TranceOne — The Loft Closer Who Went Dark at Kingdom

For three years TranceOne closed other people’s rooms. At Kingdom 2026, he opened his own — under a new name, in the dark.

KEEPITIL

On August 22, 2026, at Catch One in Los Angeles, TranceOne did the thing he’d been circling for two years. The Kingdom rave — We Love Kandy’s full-venue takeover, seventy-plus DJs across a warren of themed rooms — had been his home stage since 2024, the place where he closed the Loft with tech trance while the building shook around him. This time he walked into the Overdrive stage (the Jewels room) at midnight under a different name: V∧NT∧8L4CK, his new hard techno alias, making its debut. His recap a week later is the sound of an artist who found the door he was looking for: “Last weekend at Kingdom was absolutely incredible! I got to debut my new hard techno alias, V∧NT∧8L4CK, and this was by far the most fun I’ve had playing a set.”

That sentence lands because of everything underneath it. TranceOne (Shane Seidel) of Santa Clarita is a tech trance DJ who spent years as one of the SoCal underground’s most reliable closers — the name promoters put in the last slot of the night, 1:15 to 2 a.m., when the room needs someone who will not let it empty. In 2026 he added the hard end of the spectrum to the act, first with a debut hard techno set in March, then with a whole second identity. The trance didn’t go anywhere. It got a darker sibling.

The sound: tech trance at the core, hard techno at the edge

His application to KEEPITIL lists “Tech Trance and Hard Techno.” His Instagram bio files it slightly differently — “Tech trance/ hard trance DJ” — and his hashtags across three years of posts fill in the rest of the palette: tech trance and uplifting trance at the center, techno and hard techno at the edges, with the trance family tags (#trancefamilyla, #trancefamilysf, #trancefamilysd, #trancefamilyoc) marking the community the music is for. This is rave music in the classic SoCal sense — kandi, PLUR, themed mega-rooms — and his own January 2024 reel spells the creed out in full: “peace love unity respect.”

The hard turn was deliberate and staged in public. In March 2026 he announced the result himself: “Last weekend was incredible!! I played my first hard techno set and it was a huge success.” By April he was promising “another heavy dose of some hard techno” at Catch One, and by August the experiment had a name, a look — a visual artist built a dedicated hybrid 2D/3D visual showcase for “tranceonemusic’s new project Vantabl4ck,” designed to “hit just as hard as your music” — and a debut on the biggest underground stage he plays. Two acts, one booking: TranceOne for the trance rooms, V∧NT∧8L4CK for the dark ones.

Kingdom: the home stage

Every artist in this scene has one room that explains them. TranceOne’s is Catch One, through We Love Kandy — the promoter whose own profile counts “30+ underground raves, & nightclub events across SoCal” — and its flagship, Kingdom. The receipts run in a straight line. January 2024: his first announced event of the year, a We Love Kandy set in the Loft (the Cupcake Factory room) from 10:40 to 11:30 p.m., doubling as his birthday weekend. February 2024: back in the same Loft, same slot, birthday again — his We Love Kandy sets keep landing on his birthday, until the birthday set is practically a residency of its own (his profile photo, fittingly, shows him in a birthday sash). August 2024: Kingdom itself, and not an early slot — “I’ll be closing out the Techno Tempest stage, 1:15am - 2:00am.” His recap: “Last night was WILD! Thank you @welovekandysocal for bringing us all together for the one thing we all have in common, the love for this music and the scene. Until next time, Kingdom.”

January 2025: back for We Love Kandy in the Underground at Catch One, 9:00–9:50 p.m. August 2025: Kingdom again, the Underground, 1:10–2:00 a.m. — the closing slot, again. January 2026: the Jewels stage (the Lollipop Forest), 10:40–11:30 p.m., “come celebrate my birthday month with me out on the dance floor.” And August 2026: the V∧NT∧8L4CK debut at the Overdrive stage, after which he signed off the recap with “See you all next year!” Kingdom’s own scale explains why the streak matters: the 2025 edition took over the whole of Catch One at 4067 Pico Boulevard with over 70 artists from across SoCal; the 2026 edition billed 70+ DJs and ten immersive worlds in one venue, one night. Being a recurring name — and a recurring closer — inside a machine that size is a track record, not a favor.

Running through the whole streak is the working DJ’s other credential: his own promo code. Post after post carries it — “use code: TRANCEONE to save $5 on your tickets,” “use code Tranceone and save 20%,” “Use code: TRANCEONE at checkout” — the mark of a DJ whose name moves tickets, which is why promoters keep handing him the last hour of the night.

The Mayhem and Intricacy circuit

His bio’s second line is an affiliation: @mayhemevents — Mayhem Events, the SoCal promoter (26,000+ followers) that runs premier holiday and themed raves “from Avalon, Catch One to the Yost Theater.” His Mayhem receipt is a headline room: Memorial Day weekend 2024, at Avalon Hollywood, bringing “the tech trance in the lobby from 12:15am-1:00am” — a peak-hour lobby set at one of LA’s landmark dance venues, promoted with his TRANCEONE code.

The second circuit is Intricacy and Circle OC in Huntington Beach. June 2024: a closing set for Intricacy (“On Saturday, June 15th, I’ll be closing out the night!”) and an OC day party the following week, 3–8 p.m. July 2024: Intricacy at Circle OC, free with RSVP before 11. Then the anniversary: Intricacy’s 10 Year Anniversary ENCORE at Circle OC in September 2025, built around trance legend Roger Shah playing open-to-close, with TranceOne on the bill in the basement — the announcement’s phrase for the undercard is “bringing the underground heat all night long to the basement.” Sharing a poster with Roger Shah, on a ten-year anniversary bill, in the basement room that carries the night’s underground credibility: that’s the trance scene’s own hierarchy recognizing one of its regulars.

And in May 2026 came the set that shows the range best — May The Fourth Be With You at Catch One, a Star Wars-themed night where he took the Underground at 12:40–1:20 a.m. His giveaway post for it is pure showman: “A transmission from the Dark Side… The night awakens. The low end takes control… I’ll be running the decks 12:40–1:20AM… peak hour, no escape.” Peak hour, themed room, costume crowd, four-pack ticket giveaway with his name on the post — he doesn’t just play these nights, he promotes them like they’re his.

Space Cadets and the duo chapter

In March 2026 he announced a second collaboration format: “San Diego, you ready?! I’m super stoked to announce that I’ll be making my San Diego debut with @kismet333music as Space Cadets at Spin Nightclub from 11pm-12am!” Space Cadets — TranceOne and Kismet 333, a fellow trance DJ/producer — took the act to Spin in San Diego, and a month later his reel places Space Cadets back at Spin for Spring Resonance. A duo brand gives promoters a third way to book him: solo trance, solo hard techno alias, or the two-man trance act that travels.

  • A recurring name promoters rebook: We Love Kandy / Kingdom at Catch One across 2024, 2025 and 2026, plus Intricacy at Circle OC and a Mayhem Events bill at Avalon Hollywood (lobby, 12:15–1:00 a.m., Memorial Day weekend 2024)
  • Two acts in one booking — plus a duo: TranceOne (tech trance / uplifting), V∧NT∧8L4CK (hard techno alias, debuted at Kingdom, Aug 22, 2026, with its own visual identity), and Space Cadets, his duo with Kismet 333 (San Diego debut at Spin Nightclub)
  • He moves tickets: his own TRANCEONE promo code runs across his Kingdom, We Love Kandy and Avalon posts, and he promotes his sets with giveaways (a four-pack for May The Fourth Be With You at Catch One)
  • Scene-native credibility: support bill for Roger Shah’s open-to-close at Intricacy’s 10 Year Anniversary (Circle OC, Sept 2025); trance family roots across LA, OC, SF and San Diego; a birthday-set tradition at We Love Kandy that his crowd shows up for yearly

The quick version

TranceOne (Shane Seidel) is a Santa Clarita tech trance and hard techno DJ, and one of the SoCal underground rave circuit’s most reliable closers. A We Love Kandy and Kingdom regular at Catch One since 2024 — where his sets double as his annual birthday celebrations — he has closed Kingdom stages in the final hour of the night, played Avalon Hollywood for Mayhem Events, billed in Circle OC’s basement for Roger Shah’s Intricacy anniversary set, and launched both a duo (Space Cadets, with Kismet 333) and a hard techno alias, V∧NT∧8L4CK, which debuted at Kingdom in August 2026.
