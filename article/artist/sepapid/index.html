KEEPITIL PRESENTS

se Papid D — No Barriers, No Genre Limits

A Boyle Heights-raised house DJ who has also been the technical director making other people’s rooms work — “No barriers, no genre limits.”

KEEPITIL

Most DJs build a wall around a sound and defend it. Dean Romo — se Papid D, of Los Angeles — posted the opposite philosophy from a livestream booth in 2022: “@theLASpectrum live streams are always so fun for me. No barriers, no genre limits. Life is about having fun after all right?” Then he added the detail that tells you how he actually works: “Big shout out to all my friends who came through yesterday to spin.” His streams weren’t a solo showcase; they were a room he opened to other DJs. That is the thread that runs through everything his public record shows — a Boyle Heights kid who grew up to work both sides of the booth: DJ, producer and remixer out front, and, behind the scenes, a technical director who has built and run rooms for other people’s biggest nights.

His Instagram bio compresses the act into three lines: “House/UK/Top40 / DJ/Producer/Remixer / Click below for latest releases!” The application he filed with KEEPITIL in May 2026 is narrower — genre: House — and house is the home base. But the bio’s wider frame is the honest one, and his own posts keep proving it.

Boyle Heights first

The roots are on the record, in his own words. In March 2017 he posted a photo from a visit to the house he grew up in: “Went to go visit the house I grew up in today. It’s crazy as you get older you realize how much of your childhood you take with you. To living in #LosAngeles #LAporVida #boyleheights #losangeles_city.” Boyle Heights, East Los Angeles — that’s the ground the act stands on, and the Los Angeles tag runs through his feed the way other DJs run a logo: location tags on post after post read Los Angeles, California, a decade deep.

The Latin thread in his music is public, too, and it’s stated in his own hashtags rather than anyone’s guesswork. Announcing his plans at the top of 2023 — “2023: the year I made the website. Who knows I might release a mixtape, some remixes and singles this year” — he tagged the post #musicahispanica, #electronicaenEspañol, #electroniclatin, #latinelectro and #electrolatino, alongside #MusicDJProducer, #Turntablism and #NoSleepTillVinyl. A Los Angeles house DJ tagging his own output as Latin electronic music, in Spanish and English at once, is telling you exactly which city made him.

The producer’s bench

The “producer/remixer” half of the billing has receipts. In February 2019 he posted a studio shot of a rack of outboard hardware — the caption is a gearhead’s holiday card: “This is all celebration I need today. Now I just need to mod them all.” The hashtags name the bench: #api, #dbx, #pultec, #la2a, #universalaudio, #reviveaudio — and the trades he claims alongside them: #musicproduction, #recording, #audioengineer. That’s not a laptop-and-presets profile; that’s a man collecting and modifying the classic analog hardware of the recording studio.

The remix work surfaces in November 2023, when he teased his take on Raye’s hit “Prada”: “Raye’s chart-topping hit ‘Prada’ just got a mind-blowing switchup… Head over to your favorite streaming platforms and immerse yourself in the captivating world of ‘Prada (se Papi D Remix)’ by Raye.” The post’s hashtags place the remix’s sound — #DeepHouse and #TechnoGroove among them — which, next to the bio’s House/UK/Top40 frame, sketches the range: a house DJ whose remix work leans deep and groovy, and who is comfortable reworking a Top 40 vocal for the dancefloor. His bio link points the same direction — the Linktree in his profile is titled “new releases here!”

The other side of the booth

What separates se Papid D from most of the Connect queue is the second career folded inside the first. In January 2018 he closed out a month on the road: “Finishing 1 month on the road. Being technical director @stellaartois pop up at #sundancefilmfestival was a blast. Talk about life changing experiences, this was one for the books.” Technical director — not guest, not attendee — for a Stella Artois pop-up at the Sundance Film Festival in Park City, Utah. A year earlier he’d posted the throwback: “#tbt to that one time @sundancefilmfestival2017 where we set up and ran @taolasvegas #redcarpet #nightclub” — he and his crew set up and ran Tao Las Vegas’s red-carpet nightclub presence at Sundance 2017.

Read those two posts together and a rare profile comes into focus: a DJ who has also been the person responsible for making the room work — the sound, the setup, the show running on time at one of the most demanding festival environments in American entertainment. His personal website tells the same story from the business side: deanromodoes.me introduces Dean Romo with “After 10 years in the industry, we decided to alter direction. Now, we share our passion by helping others,” and lists the same phone number he put on his KEEPITIL application. Promoters talk about wanting artists who understand production. Se Papid D has been production.

No barriers

The livestream era gave that philosophy its purest form. The April 2022 LA Spectrum reel is a DJ describing his ideal room: no barriers, no genre limits, friends coming through to spin. It’s a small post, but it’s the mission statement his bio backs up — House, UK sounds and Top 40 in one line was never a branding accident. It’s the same instinct that tags a release announcement with Latin-electronic hashtags and puts a Raye vocal over a deep-house groove: the crowd’s night matters more than the genre fence.

And he still shows up where the culture gathers. In September 2025 he posted from Black Rock City, location-tagged at Burning Man, Nevada, with a carousel and a verdict: “Happy Burning Man… that was a 14/10 @playgroundfoundation_.” The tag points at the Playground Foundation — Carl Cox’s long-running Burning Man sound camp, a 501(c)(3) established in 2012 — which puts his desert trip inside the orbit of one of the playa’s flagship house and techno camps. The post doesn’t claim a set, and this article won’t claim one for him; what it documents is simpler and still useful to a booker: a decade into the game, he’s still traveling to where the music is taken most seriously, and rating it like a fan.

The quick version

Se Papid D (Dean Romo) is a Los Angeles house DJ, producer and remixer — Boyle Heights-raised — whose bio bills House / UK / Top 40 and whose own remix work (a 2023 rework of Raye’s “Prada”) leans deep house and techno groove. Behind the decks he carries a second trade: event production, including a technical-director credit at a Stella Artois pop-up at the Sundance Film Festival and a Tao Las Vegas red-carpet nightclub build at Sundance 2017. His stated philosophy, from his LA Spectrum livestreams, is “No barriers, no genre limits” — and his feed, from a modded analog studio bench to a 14/10 verdict on Burning Man 2025, keeps backing it up.
