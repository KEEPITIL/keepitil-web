Organizer fees & payouts

KEEPITIL · Last updated November 27, 2026

This page is for people who sell tickets on KEEPITIL. It shows what buyers pay on top of your ticket price, how that splits between Stripe and KEEPITIL, how you get paid, what happens when a ticket is refunded or disputed, and what is not allowed. Numbers come from how KEEPITIL ticketing works and from Stripe's own pages, which are linked.

1. The fee: the buyer pays it, you receive the full ticket price

At checkout the buyer pays your ticket price plus a service fee of 5% + $0.50 per order. The fee is shown to the buyer before they pay. You receive the full ticket price you set: nothing is taken out of your ticket money for fees. Free and RSVP tickets are never charged and carry no fee.

The service fee has two lines, so you can see where it goes:

LineStripe processing feeKEEPITIL operational feeTotal service fee
Card2.9% + 30¢2.1% + 20¢5% + 50¢
International card (extra)+1.5%+0.5%+2%
Currency conversion (extra)+1%+1%+2%
  • The $0.50 is charged once per order, not per ticket. The percentage is on the paid tickets in the order.
  • Currency conversion: the extra 2% is added when the tickets are priced in a currency other than US dollars. All tickets on KEEPITIL are priced in US dollars today.
  • International cards: Stripe charges +1.5% on them. KEEPITIL cannot see a card's country before the buyer pays, so the buyer pays the same 5% + $0.50 on every card and the extra international fee is not added.
  • The “KEEPITIL operational fee” is KEEPITIL's share: it covers running ticketing, payouts and support.

2. Stripe's fees, payouts and getting set up

  • Card processing: Stripe's published price is 2.9% + 30¢ per successful domestic card payment, plus 1.5% for international cards and plus 1% if currency conversion is needed (stripe.com/pricing). On KEEPITIL the buyer's service fee covers it: you pay no processing fee on ticket sales (Stripe: destination charges: the platform pays the Stripe fee on the charge).
  • Your Stripe account: when you set up payouts, KEEPITIL creates a Stripe Express account for you and sends you to Stripe to finish. You give Stripe your identity and bank details there, not to KEEPITIL. Stripe asks for information about you and, for a business, the business: for example name, address, tax ID number, date of birth, and details of beneficial owners. It may also ask for a scan of a government ID or proof of address, and it “temporarily pauses charges or payouts if the information isn't provided or verified” (Stripe: identity verification).
  • You cannot sell paid tickets until payouts are set up. Checkout refuses a paid ticket for an organizer who has not finished Stripe's setup.
  • When you are paid: by default, money from a sale builds up in your Stripe balance and is paid out to your bank on a daily rolling basis (Stripe: payouts for connected accounts). Stripe typically schedules a new account's first payout within 7 to 14 days (Stripe: payouts). Payout settings and status are managed with Stripe.

3. Refunds, disputes and chargebacks

  • Refunds: see the Refund Policy. A refund returns the buyer's ticket price to their original payment method. Stripe does not give back its own processing fee on a refund: “Stripe's processing fees from the original transaction aren't returned” (Stripe: refunds).
  • Who pays: refunds you owe because of your cancellation or your event's description, the processing fee Stripe does not return, and dispute amounts and dispute fees are the organizer's cost. KEEPITIL may take them from your payouts. This is already in the Ticket Terms (“Chargebacks and refunds may be offset against your payouts”).
  • Disputes (chargebacks): if a buyer disputes a payment with their bank, the payment is reversed right away and Stripe takes the amount plus a dispute fee from the platform account (Stripe: disputes). The ticket may be voided (Ticket Terms).

4. What is not allowed

Stripe's restrictions

All card payments on KEEPITIL run on Stripe, so Stripe's rules apply to every event and organizer. Stripe's list of prohibited and restricted businesses is at stripe.com/legal/restricted-businesses. Relevant entries:

  • Cannabis (prohibited): Stripe lists “Cannabis products” and “Cannabis dispensaries and related businesses” as prohibited. Stripe's list has no entry for ticketing or events as such. An event that sells cannabis, or that is run by a cannabis business, may be treated as a related business, and Stripe can refuse or stop payments for it. If your event involves cannabis in any way, contact info@keepitil.com before you publish paid tickets. We cannot promise Stripe will allow it.
  • CBD (restricted): CBD products with only negligible amounts of THC, per local limits, need extra review by Stripe; CBD above the legal THC limit is prohibited.
  • Gambling (prohibited): Stripe lists games of chance, including sweepstakes and contests with a monetary or material prize, and lotteries. Do not sell tickets to raffles, lotteries or prize draws.
  • Adult content (prohibited), tobacco (restricted) and anything illegal: Stripe prohibits pornography and content made for sexual gratification, restricts tobacco and e-cigarette products sold in line with the law, and prohibits illegal drugs and anything that breaks the law where you operate or sell.

Stripe can change its list. The version above was read on November 27, 2026; Stripe's page says “Last updated: 2026-09-22”. If Stripe closes or limits your account, KEEPITIL cannot reverse that.

KEEPITIL's rules

  • You must be at least 18, or the age of majority where you live, and have a KEEPITIL account (Terms). Buyers also need a free KEEPITIL account to buy a ticket.
  • You must finish Stripe's identity and bank setup before you can sell paid tickets, and have the right to sell tickets to the event (Ticket Terms, section 6).
  • Paid tickets must cost at least $1.00. Prices are in US dollars. A buyer can add up to 20 tickets of one type to an order.
  • An event must be published (new events are reviewed first), and tickets cannot be bought once the event has started or outside the sales dates you set. Sold-out tiers stop selling.
  • An event that has sold tickets cannot be deleted. Refund every ticket first; then the event can be hidden.
  • You must describe the event accurately, honor valid tickets and follow venue, licensing, safety and local laws, including age limits such as 18+ or 21+ (Ticket Terms). The Community Guidelines prohibit promoting illegal activity.
  • KEEPITIL can pause ticket sales on the whole site.
