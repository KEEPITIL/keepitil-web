KEEPITIL PRESENTS

elysium — The Name of Paradise, the Sound of Destruction

The rooms found her before the algorithm did — contest wins, a Hex Cougar support slot, and a Saturday set at Sound Haven.

KEEPITIL

Her Instagram bio is a three-line manifesto: the name of paradise, the sound of destruction, the energy of eternity — in that order. It sounds like branding until you read the booking record underneath it. The promoter spotlight for her RAVECMP Takeover set in Santa Ana puts the sound in plain language: “LA-based dubstep and bass DJ Elysium is known for explosive, high-energy sets filled with underground flips, alien wubs, unexpected drops, and crowd-favorite fakeouts. Blending rapid transitions with hard-hitting selections, she brings a sound that feels both carefully curated and ahead of the curve. Fresh off multiple open-slot wins, a support set for Hex Cougar, and a booking at Sound Haven Festival in Tennessee, Elysium is quickly making a name for herself in the West Coast bass scene.”

That spotlight was written about an LA bass DJ with 39 Instagram posts and 1,285 followers. The résumé inside it belongs to a much larger act. That gap between audience size and booking weight is the elysium story: the rooms found her before the algorithm did.

The launch: “The gates of elysium are open”

Her own launch post is four lines long: “the gates of elysium are open. / a name that doesn’t fade, a sound that doesn’t die. / welcome to eternity. / gratitude to @yourshot_USA for helping me launch this story in sound.” YourShot USA helped launch the project; everything since has been stage by stage. Her SoundCloud — under the name elysium, listing Elisabeth Tracy and Los Angeles — carries her sets, including the full Sound Haven set. Her Linktree gathers SoundCloud, YouTube, TikTok and Twitch in one place.

The contest route, won repeatedly

elysium keeps winning the open slot. She won the open slot contest for Hex Cougar: “feeling so grateful to have won the open slot contest for @hexcougarmusic — the melrose house | 9pm - 9:50pm | 4/3/26.” She won again for Pride: Bass N Babes announced two winners of its open slot contest, Fayrah and elysium, noting the mixes they submitted were “a spectacular match with the rest of our lineup,” and she played Pride for Bassheads on June 13, 9–9:50 p.m. in Burbank/Glendale. And at Building Man Festival in Utah, she was a DJ Contest runner-up earning her place on the decks, on the Pop-Up Party lineup on Thursday, April 30 at the Jenkstar Ranch. Three separate contests, three separate promoters, the same result. Open-slot wins are the bass scene’s blind audition, and she keeps passing it.

The festivals: Utah first, then Jaceland

Building Man, April 30–May 3, 2026 at the Jenkstar Ranch in Green River, Utah, was her Utah debut and her first festival performance. Her recap: “this is what following your dreams looks like — y’all really made my first festival performance so special.” Her posted Building Man tracklist shows the method in print — flip stacked on flip, the “underground flips” the spotlight promises, itemised by her own hand.

Then Sound Haven. Her announcement: “i’m so unbelievably stoked to announce that i will be performing @soundhavenfestival 2026! it’s insanely wild to me to see my name up there with so many heavy-hitters that i’ve looked up to for years… 4 magical days packed full of bass, dubstep, downtempo, & dnb at Jaceland… July 30th - August 2nd!” She played the Kouch Stage on Saturday, through Sacred Hive and Kouch Kollective, and her recap closes the loop: “THANK YOU for making my first @soundhavenfestival so magical — my full set is OUT NOW on soundcloud.” A first-year festival DJ does not get a Saturday Kouch Stage set at a sound-system festival by accident.

The West Coast circuit underneath

In Santa Ana she played direct support for Circa.wav (11 p.m.–12 a.m.), headlined the RAVECMP Takeover 2.0 bill at 2222 W 2nd St on August 8, opened the bass stage at Groove Garden for her Orange County debut, and is billed for MOOD$WINGZ Halloween: The Other Side — “bass music all nite.” In San Diego she played support for Flozone and Cozy Kev over Thanksgiving weekend at 3oz Dive Club. In Pomona she played Space Taco’s Bass Taco at The Basement. On the Fourth of July she opened Independence Bass at the Dirt Dog Compound with a heavy dubstep set, promising “a bit of a heavier set than you usually get from elysium.”

In Los Angeles, at Catch One she plays two ways: solo — a November 15 date announced as “come test your neck strength & find out what elysium bass therapy feels like” — and as half of pølysium, her back-to-back project, throwing down “nasty bass, heavy dubstep, & a lil bit of riddim” at the Wubassic Park Stage. In Hollywood, Bass Garden at Dirty Laundry has booked her solo and back-to-back with DJ Pølano. And at Bass City afterhours she was billed as Bass Kitty b2b Elysium under headliner BLVCK JESUS. Solo, b2b, duo project, direct support, contest winner, festival stage: the formats keep changing because promoters keep finding new ways to use the same reliability.

The sound, in her own shorthand

Her reels are a working DJ’s lab notes — “dubstep is my satisfaction,” “gimme that weird shiii,” “kesha but make it dubstep,” “mercy but make it gross,” “served this one up ice cold” — tagged, again and again, riddim dubstep, wubz, bass face. It is flip culture documented in real time. Combined with the spotlight’s language — underground flips, alien wubs, unexpected drops and fakeouts, rapid transitions — the identity is unusually legible for an act this early: high-energy bass and dubstep, built on flips and fakeouts, played fast, aimed directly at the headbang. Her application lists the genres in four words — Bass & dubstep — and the short version and the long version say exactly the same thing.

The quick version

elysium (Elisabeth Tracy) is an LA-based bass and dubstep DJ whose three-line manifesto — the name of paradise, the sound of destruction, the energy of eternity — is backed by a booking record well ahead of her audience size: open-slot contest wins for Hex Cougar and Bass N Babes Pride, a DJ Contest runner-up slot at Building Man Festival in Utah (her first festival performance), and a Saturday Kouch Stage set at Sound Haven Festival 2026 in Tennessee with the full set live on SoundCloud. With direct support for Circa.wav, the pølysium b2b project at Catch One LA, and a continuous Santa Ana–Los Angeles–San Diego–Pomona circuit, she is “quickly making a name for herself in the West Coast bass scene.”
