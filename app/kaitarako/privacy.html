Kai Tarako is a free letter-tracing app for young children, made by KEEPITIL. This page explains, in plain words, what the app does with information. The short version: we don’t collect anything. Nothing your child does in the app is sent to us or to anyone else.

What we collect

Nothing. Kai Tarako has no accounts, no sign-in and no forms. It never asks for a name, age, email, photo, voice recording or location. It does not use the camera or the microphone.

What stays on your device

So your child can pick up where they left off, the app saves a small amount of information only on this device:

  • the settings a grown-up chooses (help level, mode, starting step and similar options);
  • simple progress counts for each lesson: how many times it was finished, how often the “?” help button was tapped, and the date it was last practised;
  • a simple skill record for each letter, word or sound (for example “trace:a”): how many times it was done, how many of those needed help (a second try or tracing help), how many were right first time on their own, and the dates (day only, at most the last 60) on which it was right on their own;
  • the date progress was first saved.

The app does not save the drawings or finger paths your child makes. This information never leaves the device unless you choose to save a backup.

Backups

In the Grown-ups area you can Save backup to make a file with the progress and settings above. The file goes wherever you choose to save it on your device. We never receive it. Restore backup reads such a file back in. The app checks the file and ignores anything that isn’t a Kai Tarako backup.

Nothing sent, no ads, no tracking

  • No advertising of any kind.
  • No analytics, no tracking and no device identifiers.
  • No third-party code, services or software kits. The app makes no network requests while your child uses it.
  • No in-app purchases, chat, social features or links for your child to follow.

Speech is the device’s built-in voices. Letters and prompts are read aloud on the phone by those voices. Nothing is recorded, and no audio is sent anywhere.

The Grown-ups area (parental gate)

Settings, backups, this policy and anything that leaves the app sit behind a grown-ups gate. To open it, press and hold the Grown-ups button for 3 seconds, then answer a times-table question (for example, 6 × 7). After two wrong answers the gate locks for one minute. This keeps young children out; it is not a password and does not identify anyone.

Deleting information

  • Reset in the app: Grown-ups → Privacy → Delete all progress removes all progress and settings from this device.
  • Remove the app: deleting Kai Tarako from your device removes everything it saved.
  • Any backup files you saved stay where you put them until you delete them.

Because we hold no information about you or your child, there is nothing for us to delete on our side.

Children’s privacy

Kai Tarako is made for children. We built it to collect no personal information from children.

Parents’ rights

A parent or guardian decides whether a child uses Kai Tarako. Because the app collects no personal information, KEEPITIL has no account, profile, or file about your child to show you, correct, or delete.

On the device, a parent can:

  • Review the settings and progress counts described above. They are in the Grown-ups area, on this device only.
  • Delete them with Grown-ups → Privacy → Delete all progress.
  • Remove the app, which deletes everything it saved on the device.
  • Keep or delete any backup file you saved. We never receive that file.

Questions about your child’s use of the app: email info@keepitil.com.

Web version

If you use Kai Tarako in a web browser instead of the app, the files are delivered by GitHub Pages. Like any web host, GitHub receives standard technical information (such as your IP address and browser type) under GitHub’s own privacy statement. KEEPITIL does not see or keep these logs.

Changes

If this policy changes, we will update this page and its date. We will not start collecting information without changing this policy first and, where the law requires it, asking a parent.
