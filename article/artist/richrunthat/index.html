KEEPITIL PRESENTS

Richrunthat — The Vinyl Kid Who Came Back to Run the Room

Plenty of DJs come back. Richrunthat came back, took the residencies, the rooftops and the warehouse openers — and got named his scene’s first headliner.

KEEPITIL

In August 2026, 6HOST Records did something it had never done: it named a headliner. Not booked one — named one, the way a school names a graduating class. “RICHRUNTHAT HAS OFFICIALLY BEEN SELECTED AS THE INAUGURAL HEADLINER OF HEADLINER ACADEMY — CLASS I,” the announcement read. “Not because he was given the title. Because he earned it.” The room was Proof Bar in Santa Ana, the date was Thursday, August 20, and the DJ being inducted was an Inglewood producer who, fifteen years earlier, had learned to mix on vinyl — then walked away from the decks for eight years.

That arc — vinyl kid, long silence, full-time comeback — is the whole Richrunthat story in one line. Richrunthat (Richard Barahona) plays tech house, acid techno and hard techno, holds residencies with Duck Pond LA and Redline Records, and in the span of a single year went from returning DJ to the first name on his scene’s headliner roster. His own website puts the offer plainly: “Producer and DJ delivering high-energy tech house and hard techno sets.”

Learned on vinyl, lost to the pause

His most complete biography is one he posted himself, in the VOLUME 031 feature his feed carried in May 2025: “RICH RUN THAT is a passionate Tech House and Techno DJ with a rich musical background rooted in 90’s hip hop. He learned to DJ on vinyl 15 years ago, honing his craft on Technics 1200MK5s paired with a Vestax PMC-05 Pro. After an 8-year hiatus, he rediscovered his love for DJing two years ago and made the bold decision to pursue his dream of becoming a full-time DJ. Known for his seamless mixing and deep grooves, RICH RUN THAT delivers an authentic and energetic vibe with every set.”

Read that gear list again. Technics 1200MK5s and a Vestax PMC-05 Pro are a hip-hop DJ’s tools — a battle-era setup, learned by hand, with no sync button to lean on. The ’90s hip-hop root explains a lot about what came later: the edits catalogue is built from Dr. Dre, Juicy J and Bad Bunny, flipped into tech house weapons. The eight-year hiatus explains the rest — the hunger in the current run doesn’t read like a hobbyist’s. It reads like a man making up for a decade.

The sound: three lanes, one driver

His application to KEEPITIL lists tech house and acid techno; his Instagram bio widens it to “Tech House/ Acid Techno / Hard Techno,” and his website’s About section states the mission in his own words: “I’m Rich, a producer and DJ creating high-energy sets across tech house, acid techno, and hard techno. I play for listeners, dancers, and event teams looking for a bold underground sound with real crowd connection. From edits and original production to live DJ sets, I bring intensity, rhythm, and a personal edge to every room.”

The lanes show up as distinct bookings, not just bio copy. The tech house side is the Breach Fridays and Space Taco Tuesdays side — groovy, vocal, edit-heavy. The acid side is the Framed Events side: a midnight rooftop set in LA (“Had so much fun playing Acid Techno on a rooftop in LA! Thank you @framed.events for having me!”) and a 4 a.m. set he billed himself with “Give me some f**king Acid techno” and the hashtag #DemonTime. The hard techno side is the warehouse side: his September 2026 debut for Tech My Warehouse LA’s WIDE AWAKE, where he took the 10 p.m. opening set on the hard techno stage — “I’m setting the tone for WIDE AWAKE all night” — and a week later posted the opener’s creed: “Opening a warehouse rave hits different.”

Resident, not guest

His bio carries the line promoters look for first: “Resident DJ @duckpxndla / @redlinerecords.us.” Duck Pond LA (Duckpxnd) bills itself “HARD TECHNO | HOUSE | & MORE,” and Richrunthat is woven through its 2026 calendar — a 1–2 a.m. set in May, a midnight set the following week plus the 3–4:30 a.m. closing back-to-back with RAVE GOD, a 2–3 a.m. slot at ENTRAPMENT, the opening slot at ERROR in June, and a 4–5 a.m. close he announced as “closing out the night with industrial.” With Redline Records — a house and techno events label — he plays the Rave & Brew series with Floored Entertainment at Dayli Coffee, including a 12:35 a.m. night-edition set.

Residency is a different credential from a guest slot. A guest gets booked for a night; a resident gets trusted with the room’s shape — opening it, closing it, and going back-to-back with the headliner in the final ninety minutes. His Duck Pond run covers all three jobs.

The circuit

Around the residencies sits a working SoCal underground circuit. Breach Fridays is family — Space Taco’s own birthday post for him notes he comes “straight from our Breach Fridays family,” and his posted Breach tracklists are a signature in miniature: his own Dr. Dre “Let’s Get High” edit and ATC “Around The World” edit next to Chris Lorenzo, HNTR, Odd Mob and FOVOS, plus his unreleased original “Cocaine” tested mid-set. Space Taco Tuesdays in Pomona gave him a debut that doubled as his birthday set in August 2026 — “Tonight, Rich is throwing down a special birthday set AND making his Space Taco debut!” — and his own recap tracklist leads with his original “Alcoholic” and his Bad Bunny “Soy Peor” edit.

The rest of the map: Proof Bar in Santa Ana, repeatedly, with the 6HOST circle (including the July 2026 TMZEE takeover bill he shared with fellow KEEPITIL applicant Smoke); HOLYSH#T × 6HOST nights at 1926 E 7th Place in DTLA, where his back-to-back with RAVE GOD drew his favorite review — his own: “One of the best feelings of DJ is getting crowd reactions like this”; a DTLA rooftop for Teknlgy and Human Saint at Somewhere Special LA; Helipad Society’s SHUFFLES, where he sits at the top of the flyer; an NYE 2026 afters lineup in Orange County alongside RAVE GOD and TMZEE; and a beach day in July 2026 — the KEEPITIL Summer gathering at Magnolia & CA-1 — where he played his Bad Bunny edit on the sand and “ended the night with a crazy techno b2b2b” with Lyk N Key and RAVE GOD. In July he was also campaigning, link in bio, for votes to become the Splash House opener — the hustle running in every direction at once.

And then the Headliner Academy induction in August, which is what a year of that circuit adds up to. 6HOST’s citation is specific about why: “After a year of watching artists grow, evolve, take risks, command rooms, and help build the 6H0ST movement…” He didn’t just play the scene’s rooms; he helped build the movement that runs them, and the movement said so on the record.

The production pivot

The most telling post in his feed might be the quiet one from June 2026: “Haven’t recorded a tech house set in months since I’ve been putting all my focus on production. Here’s a new set with a bunch of my personal ID’s.” The IDs became a catalogue. Originals out on all platforms include “Alcoholic,” “Illegal Export” and “Drug Addiction.” The edit run — most of it posted to SoundCloud as free downloads, five of them pinned in his Instagram bio links — reworks Kaskade’s “Angel On My Shoulder” (his pinned link), Bad Bunny’s “Soy Peor,” Dr. Dre’s “Let’s Get High,” Juicy J’s “Bandz A Make Her Dance,” ATC’s “Around The World” and Calvin Harris’s “One Kiss” (an acid edit). The workflow is documented in his reels: “Straight from #FLStudio to the USB and SoundCloud.”

In July he set the pace publicly — “Dropping music weekly till a label notices me” — and in October he was on the radio side of the craft, interviewed by Alley Kat on UGN Rave Radio. The clip he chose to share is the producer’s confession every producer recognizes: “You finish a session in the middle of the night thinking you just made the best song of your life… wake up the next morning, press play, and it sounds like a completely different track.” That’s not a DJ talking about other people’s records. That’s a producer talking about his own.

Why book Richrunthat

  • Residency-tested: resident DJ for Duck Pond LA and Redline Records — trusted to open (WIDE AWAKE hard techno stage, 10 p.m.), close (Duck Pond 4–5 a.m. industrial close) and go b2b with headliners in the final slot
  • A named headliner: selected by 6HOST Records as the inaugural Headliner Academy — Class I headliner (Proof Bar, Santa Ana, Aug 20, 2026) — “Not because he was given the title. Because he earned it.”
  • Three bookable lanes in one DJ: tech house (Breach Fridays, Space Taco Tuesdays), acid techno (Framed Events rooftops and afters), hard techno (Tech My Warehouse LA, Duck Pond)
  • A producer’s set, not just a selector’s: originals on all platforms (“Alcoholic,” “Illegal Export,” “Drug Addiction”) plus a SoundCloud free-download edit catalogue (Kaskade, Bad Bunny, Dr. Dre, Juicy J, ATC) that he tests in his own sets
  • Vinyl foundation: learned on Technics 1200MK5s with a ’90s hip-hop background — “seamless mixing and deep grooves,” per his VOLUME 031 bio — and a full-time DJ since his return, interviewed on UGN Rave Radio

The quick version

Richrunthat (Richard Barahona) is an Inglewood producer and DJ working tech house, acid techno and hard techno. He learned to DJ on vinyl fifteen years ago, took an eight-year hiatus, and came back two years ago to do it full-time — since then he has become a resident for Duck Pond LA and Redline Records, a Breach Fridays and Space Taco regular, a Framed Events and Tech My Warehouse LA player, and, in August 2026, 6HOST Records’ inaugural Headliner Academy headliner. His originals (“Alcoholic,” “Illegal Export,” “Drug Addiction”) and his hip-hop-flip edit catalogue are the sound; the residencies are the proof.
