KEEPITIL PRESENTS

ONEXSHOT — Just Watch Me

His bio is two lines: a dare — “Just watch me” — and a SoundCloud link. Twenty-two reels later, the dare is holding.

KEEPITIL

His entire Instagram bio is two lines long. The first is a dare: “Just watch me.” The second is a link to his SoundCloud. That is the whole pitch — no booking agent, no label name, no list of rooms played — and the feed underneath it spends twenty-two reels backing the dare up. ONEXSHOT — Daniel Robles, of Northridge, in the San Fernando Valley — is a tech house and techno producer who releases music the way other people post stories: constantly, publicly, and with the working still showing. Un-mastered. Un-mixed. Dropped anyway.

The clearest proof came in a single week of July 2026, when his feed turned into a release schedule. On July 10 he announced “JUST DROPPED 🫳 FILTHY MUSIC. WE'VE BEEN WAITING ALL DAY LONG.” On July 15, two drops in one day: “ROLLING DEEP. DROPPING TONIGHT ON ALL PLATFORMS” in the afternoon, “BAD INFLUENCE WILL BE DROPPED TODAY ON ALL PLATFORMS” right behind it. On July 16 came “FLOW,” with the method confessed in the caption: “All my recent tracks have been UN-MASTERED & MIX and are being dropped from my old laptop that I know longer use.” And on July 17, the summary of the whole run: “DONT YOU WANT TO PLAY. WILL BE DROPPED TOMORROW 🫳 SO MANY UN-MASTERED & UN-MIXED SONGS I MADE IN ONE WEEK. FINALLY BEING ABLE TO SHOW THEM OFF. ENJOY GANG.”

Most producers sit on finished tracks for months waiting for the perfect rollout. ONEXSHOT found an old laptop, emptied it onto every platform in a week, and told everybody exactly what he was doing. That is not a marketing strategy so much as a personality — and it is the same personality the name describes. One shot. No second take.

The sound

The application he filed with KEEPITIL in June 2026 lists his genres plainly: tech house and techno. His own hashtags add the rest of the fingerprint — #techhouse and #818valley on post after post, and #ableton, naming the software the tracks are built in. The 818 tag is not decoration. It is the San Fernando Valley's area code and his home turf — Northridge — and he wears it the way other DJs wear a label logo: on everything.

The catalogue backs the tags. The streaming listing for “onexshot” — genre-tagged House — runs ten deep at the top: “little taste,” “Take Control,” “Say It,” “Feel My Bass,” “Candy Store,” “new phone,” “dont call me,” “Dimelo,” “Can We Get Down,” and “It Starts.” The titles alone sketch the record collection: bass-forward tech house with a Latin edge on “Dimelo,” vocal-hook records like “Say It” and “dont call me,” and plain declarations of intent like “Feel My Bass.” His own descriptions stay in the same register — the newest one, “What You Gonna Do,” announced October 6, 2026, is billed in his caption as “That filthy tech house,” and its promo visual labels it exactly what it is: “TECH HOUSE — ORIGINAL MIX.”

The catalogue, drop by drop

ONEXSHOT's public timeline starts in the spring of 2024, and it reads like a producer's notebook left open. The earliest reel on his grid, March 20, 2024, is a track preview pointed at his SoundCloud (“I just wanna 🎶 — Onexshot -soundcloud”). A month later came “Activate.” Through 2024 and 2025 the feed keeps the same rhythm: an unreleased I.D teased in December 2024 (“Unreleased i.d coming out soon !”), another I.D in April 2025, “Nothing But Gas” in August 2025.

The record that ties the catalogue together is “Take Control.” He teased it as an I.D on October 14, 2025 — the reel's visual is a graffiti-covered alley wall with TAKE CONTROL sprayed across it and “by ONEXSHOT” signed underneath, street art as a release strategy. The streaming services now list “Take Control” as his latest single. The wall, it turns out, was the announcement.

Then there is the audience's pick. On March 26, 2026, he posted: “My most requested song is finally being dropped tonight at midnight ! Thank you guys for all the support and feedback 🙏 — ONEXSHOT - SOO FINEE.” A “most requested song” is a small phrase with a big implication: people were asking for his unreleased music by name, in enough numbers that he could rank the requests. Eight hundred and thirty-three followers is not a stadium — but it is a room where the crowd knows the I.Ds.

And the run is not finished. His most recent reel, October 6, 2026, announces “What You Gonna Do — Coming out this month!” The old laptop may be empty. The producer isn't.

The rooms

For a producer whose public life is mostly release reels, ONEXSHOT's live receipts are specific. On June 20, 2026, he played SFV SUMMER — an all-ages daytime event, 3 p.m. to 10 p.m., at Woodley Park in the San Fernando Valley, on a BASSBOSS rig, on a bill with Marlon, Vicsosa, DJ Tensión B2B Jaylyric, Dr. Defiant, Darweeezyyy and Casper, under the SECRET and DINNER CIRCLE banners. A park, a stack of BASSBOSS boxes, and a hometown crowd: that is the Valley's underground in its natural habitat, and ONEXSHOT was on the flyer. He posted that flyer on June 16, 2026 — the same day his KEEPITIL application is dated.

The summer before, he crossed the county line for KEEPITIL's own beach party: KEEPITIL SUMMER 2025, Saturday, July 26, 2025, in Huntington Beach, 8 p.m. to 2 a.m., where the flyer bills ONEXSHOT alongside OSCAR MARIN, SMOKE, RAVE GOD, RICHRUNTHAT and more, with tickets through Posh and Eventbrite. Two of this platform's own Connect subjects were on that stage with him — which makes this feature less an introduction than a reunion.

The rest of his education happened on the other side of the decks, and his feed is honest about it. A May 2025 reel from EDC carries the caption “#edc2025” over festival fireworks, and the words on the video say what the floor does to him: “I finally had the best time of my life. Thank youuu!” A 2024 reel documents Nocturnal Wonderland. ONEXSHOT studies the big rooms as a fan first — then goes home to Northridge and builds his own versions in Ableton.

The quick version

ONEXSHOT (Daniel Robles) is a Northridge tech house and techno producer from the San Fernando Valley — the 818 he hashtags on everything — with a ten-track streaming catalogue (“Take Control,” “Feel My Bass,” “new phone,” “Dimelo”), a fan-requested single in “SOO FINEE,” and a July 2026 run that saw him empty an old laptop of un-mastered tracks onto every platform in one week. He has played SFV SUMMER at Woodley Park and KEEPITIL SUMMER 2025 in Huntington Beach, and he applied to KEEPITIL in June 2026 to take the next rooms. His bio is the whole review: “Just watch me.”
