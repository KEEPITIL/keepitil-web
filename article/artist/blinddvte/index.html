KEEPITIL PRESENTS

BLIND DVTE — From the EDC Crowd in 2009 to Three Sets Under the Electric Sky

Plenty of DJs attend a festival for years before they play it. BLIND DVTE attended EDC in 2009 — and in 2026 he played it three times in one weekend.

KEEPITIL

In May 2026, BLIND DVTE played Electric Daisy Carnival Las Vegas three times in one weekend. Friday with Insomniac Passport, Saturday at 4:00–5:00 a.m. on the Kandies World stage inside Rainbow Bazaar, and Sunday at Camp EDC with the Camp OG family who had invited him in. Then he wrote the line that explains the whole arc: “I attended EDC 2009 for the first time back when it was in Los Angeles and playing 3 sets in 2026 was a full circle moment.” Seventeen years from the crowd to the booth — three times over, in a single weekend.

That is the shape of this Los Angeles house DJ’s record. BLIND DVTE — Estevan, per his KEEPITIL application, which lists exactly one genre: House — is not a festival tourist who got lucky once. He is a working piece of Insomniac’s camp-and-lounge ecosystem, a DTLA rooftop and afters regular, and a producer who gives most of his music away as free SoundCloud downloads while he stacks the next set time. The festivals are the headline. The circuit underneath them is the story.

The full-circle weekend

Start with the weekend itself, because he documented every step of it. On May 4, 2026, the announcement was pure disbelief: “MOM IM PLAYING CAMP EDC!” — “FROM ATTENDING CAMP OG THESE PAST YEARS TO PLAYING THIS YEAR IS SURREAL!” The Camp OG connection is the thread: he had been attending with that crew for years before they put him on. By the Saturday of the festival he was announcing a 4:00–5:00 a.m. set on the Kandies World stage inside Rainbow Bazaar — “IM BRINGING THE UNDERGROUND AFTERS VIBE TO EDC!” — which is exactly the right self-description: an afters DJ’s hour, played an afters DJ’s way, inside the biggest festival in the country.

His recap, posted May 21, counts the full run: “3 days, 3 Sets and each set will forever be a memorable one,” with thanks to Insomniac Events and Insomniac Passport for Friday, Kandies World for Saturday, and Camp EDC, Camp OG and Trever Britt for Sunday’s invitation. Three different doors into the same festival — the Passport lounge, a stage, and the campgrounds — is not a booking pattern you get from a press kit. It’s what happens when the people who run the side rooms already know your name.

And EDC wasn’t the end of the festival run. In September 2026 he was back with the same family at Nocturnal Wonderland, playing Camp OG on Saturday from 4:00–5:00 p.m. and posting the day-of call-out: “If you’re camping for NOCTURNAL make sure to stop by CAMP OG stage for some groovy vibes.” His thank-you afterwards was addressed to the institution, not the crowd: “Huge shoot out to @insomniacevents CAMP OG for the invite and having me play their stage at Camp! Really appreciate everyone who came out early for my set.”

The Insomniac circuit

Read his feed as a map and a pattern emerges: Insomniac’s fringe is his home turf, and he works all of it. At Escape Halloween he played the Insomniac Passport lounge from 11:00 p.m. to midnight — “See you on the dance floor!!” — and an earlier Escape reel calls it plainly: “I got to play my first festival! @escapehalloween let’s do that all over again this year? Huge shout out @veopgroup for having me.” At Day Trip LA he played the Insomniac Passport members lounge at 10 p.m., offering to bring a few commenters in with him to experience the Passport lounge during his set, and his recap thanks the Passport team “for always inviting me out to play for their amazing lounge and stage.” That word — always — is doing real work. These are repeat invitations, not one-offs.

The busy-weekend post from the summer of 2026 shows how the layers stack in a single run: July 30, TEKNLGY × Camp OG at Somewhere Special in DTLA; July 31, Framed Events at a secret DTLA location; August 1, HARD Summer at SoFi Stadium in Inglewood. Rooftop, warehouse, stadium — three nights, three formats, one city. Add the San Jose trip, where a reel has him playing Wobbleland Fest on the Kandies World stage from 10:00–11:00 p.m., and the “Los Angeles DJ” label starts to look like a home-base description rather than a boundary.

Rooftops, warehouses and the afters economy

Between festivals, BLIND DVTE’s calendar is the DTLA underground’s working week. The rooftops: Quantum Groove at Somewhere Special, where his March 22, 2026 set ran 7:00–8:00 p.m. under billing that promised “Latin & tech house, rooftop energy” over the downtown skyline, and where Quantum Groove’s own copy groups him with “heavy hitters” who have “lit up stages at EDC and festivals worldwide.” The Compound LA, where he played TEKNLGY and Bear Cave Events’ Casa de Tek night in March 2026 alongside a stacked lineup. Vulsar LA, where the venue’s own post billed him as the artist who “takes the stage dropping original bangers all night” and his own slot ran 1:00–2:00 a.m. Code Red LA in DTLA, an 11:00 p.m. opening set. Apt 503 in Koreatown, 10:00–11:00 p.m. — “PULL UP FOR SOME CASITA.”

Then the afters, which are his natural habitat: Recollect Afters in DTLA from midnight to 1:00 a.m.; Underwar3, where he played 2:00–3:00 a.m. on the Fourth of July and came back for Thanksgiving weekend’s leftovers shift, 1:15–2:30 a.m., promising to serve “unreleased beats”; Framed Events’ Friday Night Afters bills; and a day-and-night DTLA party for Privadas con el 8 where he closed the night out — “pull up for all the Latin vibes.”

The map extends past LA when the call comes. In San Diego, at The Ballroom in the Gaslamp, he played what he himself called “my San Diego Debut” — “We brought the LA Underground house vibes to SD!” In Bakersfield, he played Hyde Underground’s discovery stage from 10:00–11:00 p.m. on a show he announced as officially sold out, and his recap was still buzzing: “That was a whole vibe yall popped out and brought the energy all set.” Discovery stage to sold-out room is a promoter story as much as a DJ story.

The sound — and the giveaway economy

His application lists one genre, House, and his own posts keep widening it honestly: house and tech house in the hashtags, Latin & tech house on the Quantum Groove billing, minimal house in his own track-talk reels, and a producer’s catalogue that treats SoundCloud as a free record store for his crowd.

The catalogue, as he announced it: the single “Don’t Stop,” out in September 2026 and “available to stream on all platforms! FREE DOWNLOAD exclusively on my Sound Cloud!” The 420 freebie “Get High” — “Here’s a new freebie for ya on 420!” — again a SoundCloud stream-and-download. A remix of John Summit’s “Lights Go Out,” released on SoundCloud in February 2026 with a free download. A remix of Subtronics and Inez’s “Eyes Cut Deeper,” dropped in the same breath as his EDC Saturday announcement. And the collaborative one: “Chanel,” a collab EP with CC Love featuring San Diego’s Santa Mykah, released December 27, 2024 on all streaming platforms, announced by his collaborator as “My collab EP ‘Chanel’ w/ @blinddvte_music featuring San Diego’s very own @santamykah.”

The pattern across all of it is deliberate generosity. Singles stream everywhere, but the free download lives on his SoundCloud — the link his Instagram bio points to through his Linktree, under the simplest bio in this queue: “Producer/Dj, Los Angeles, Ca, Find my music here.” It is a funnel built by a working DJ: the festivals and the guestlist posts bring people in, the free music gives them something to take home.

The person in the posts

The bio opens with three Mexican flags, and the Latin thread in his bookings — the Privadas closing set, the Latin & tech house rooftop billing, “CASITA” in Koreatown — is his own positioning, not a writer’s invention. The rest of the personality is right there in the feed: an LAFC supporter (“Dale BLACK & GOLD!” in a 3252-tagged reel, and an LAFC Football Club shirt in his profile photo, taken in front of his own neon BLIND DVTE sunset backdrop). His birthday is October 5 — his own post marks it “DOB: 10/05” — and the rooftop crew at Somewhere Special folded his birthday celebration into a Space Taco Tuesdays week the following October.

None of that is filler. It is why the Camp OG family language fits him: his posts call that crew “my CAMP OG FAMILIA” and “the CAMP OG family,” and the invitations keep coming back because, in that corner of the scene, showing up for years is the credential.
